European Legends — Office Adventure Game

An independent balance & design review of the ruleset, ahead of an economic simulation of the trading and crafting system.

Ruleset v.16 Aug 2026 8 guilds · 40 players 100-minute economy

This is a written pass only — no code has been run yet. Findings are grouped by severity/type: structural risks, economic balance, timing, rules gaps, operational risk, and what's already working well.

Risk1. Room scheduling has no coordination mechanism

This is the single largest risk in the current draft.

Each round, 4 activity rooms × 2 guild slots = exactly 8 slots — one for each guild. Over 4 rounds that's 32 slots, precisely enough for all 8 guilds to visit all 4 rooms once each. But nothing in the rules guarantees this ideal matching actually happens. Guilds choose (or race for) rooms themselves each round, constrained only by "two other guilds already paid their participation fee" locking a room. With 8 semi-informed teams converging on the Guildhall simultaneously every 10 minutes, decentralized self-scheduling can easily produce collisions — one guild shut out of a room it still needs, in every remaining round.

Consequence: a guild can end the game having visited only 2–3 of the 4 rooms, permanently missing ~20–25 coins of quest income per skipped room, on top of being funneled into avoidable loans (§2). This is the default outcome of unmanaged parallel demand on a hard 8-slots/round cap, not a rare edge case.

Recommendation

Pre-assigned rotation (safest): print each guild a fixed 4-round room order on their team sheet, designed as a Latin square so every room has exactly 2 guilds every round, guaranteed. This removes the coordination problem entirely and costs nothing to implement. An alternative — a visible booking board at the Guildhall — is lower design effort but reintroduces the race/coordination risk.

Balance2. Economic balance

2.1 Round-1 starting kits and the recipe graph

The four Tier-1 materials form a 4-cycle in the conversion chart: Flax–Wax → Candle, Wax–Charcoal → Dye, Charcoal–Saltpetre → Black Powder, Saltpetre–Flax → Cloth. Diagonal pairs (Flax+Charcoal, Wax+Saltpetre) don't convert to anything.

Each guild starts with 3 random different Tier-1 items — missing exactly one of the four types. Removing one node from a 4-cycle always leaves a 3-node path with a shared middle item, so a guild's starting hand can always craft exactly one Tier-2 item — never zero, never two. A nice, deliberate-feeling property, worth confirming it was intentional.

The catch: which Tier-2 item a guild can make depends on which Tier-1 type is missing from their random deal. If the deal is purely random per guild, there's no guarantee the 8 guilds split evenly across the 4 possible outcomes — e.g. 5 guilds able to make only Cloth and 1 able to make Black Powder, which starves Room 3 and overloads Room 1 the moment §1's dynamics kick in.

Recommendation: don't leave the missing item to pure chance. Deliberately deal starting kits so exactly 2 guilds are missing each Tier-1 type — same "feels random" experience for players, but Round-1 room demand then exactly matches Room-1 capacity, and it makes the Latin-square rotation in §1 trivial to design consistently.

2.2 The loan mechanic can snowball without a catch-up path

A guild that can't pay a room's entry fee and lacks 15 coins takes a GM loan for the shortfall, repaid at double value at game end. Reasonable as a safety valve, but it has no rubber-banding: a guild behind on luck (§2.1, §1) needs loans more often in later rounds too, and each loan digs deeper — a round-4 loan costs the same 30 coins as a round-1 loan, but leaves no time to earn it back. A guild can be mathematically out of contention by round 2 without any player mistake.

That matters more here than in a purely competitive game — a team locked out of winning by minute 30 (of 100) with no misplay of their own tends to check out, undercutting the stated team-building/entertainment purpose.

Recommendation: cap total loan interest (e.g. double only on the first loan, flat on subsequent ones), or let each guild's unique quest (15–20 coins) function as an explicit catch-up lever regardless of Guildhall standing.

2.3 The crafting economy rewards conversion — good design

Selling prices (Tier 1 = 1, Tier 2 = 4, Tier 3 = 14, guild special Tier 3 = 20) mean two Tier-1 items (2 coins raw) into one Tier-2 (4 coins) is a "free" 100% value gain, and two Tier-2 into one Tier-3 (8 → 14) is another +75%. This is the right incentive shape: crafting is worth doing whenever a guild has spare time and matching items. It only pays off in practice if the chain is reachable within 100 minutes — the simulation should confirm that.

2.4 Guild special items and unique quests are marked "in elaboration"

The 20-coin guild-special-item bonus and each guild's unique 15–20 coin quest are central to both balance (§2.2 leans on them for catch-up) and identity, but aren't defined yet. Worth finishing before the simulation, since results will shift once guild-specific asymmetries are added.

Gaps4. Rules ambiguities worth resolving first

  • Room assignment mechanism — free choice/race, GM-directed, or fixed rotation? The current text implies racing, which drives §1.
  • Where/when can peer-to-peer trading happen? Crafting is explicitly Guildhall-only; general trades aren't location-restricted in the text — mid-activity-room, or only at trading breaks?
  • Common Quest 3B (Hall of Legends) doesn't state its clue count, unlike the other four "identify 10 things" quests.
  • Does a loan grant free entry, or just cover the coin shortfall — is the fee coins-only once converted to a loan?
  • Fallback for a guild that never visits a room — any end-game recourse, or is that quest income simply lost?

Ops5. The Guildhall is a single point of congestion

Conversions, quest-fee payments, and loans all funnel through the Guildhall/Game Master. Peer-to-peer trades appear self-administered, which helps — but crafting and fee payment do not. With up to 8 guilds hitting the Guildhall at the start of every 10-minute break, and only ~10 helpers total (most likely staffing the 4×2 activity sub-quest stations), there may be only 1–2 people free to process 8 teams in 10 minutes.

Recommendation: staff 2–3 helpers at the Guildhall during trading breaks, and consider a self-service swap table for straightforward conversions (2 cards for 1, no GM needed) so the GM only handles loans and disputes.

Working well6. What's working well

  • The 4-cycle recipe graph forces genuine cross-guild trading by design — directly serves the "networking" goal.
  • The tier price ladder makes crafting strictly worth doing without being mandatory.
  • Per-room quest timing comfortably fits the 10-minute slots when run in parallel.
  • Room-level win bonus / tie handling (5 coins, or 2/2 on a tie) is simple with no weird edge cases.
  • Charity ticket design (fixed €20, no refund) is clean and doesn't interact with the in-game economy.

7. Priority order for fixes

  1. Fix room scheduling (§1) — highest risk to the event; cheapest fix (pre-printed rotation).
  2. Control starting-kit distribution (§2.1) — free fix, also makes #1 easier.
  3. Decide the loan catch-up question (§2.2) — affects whether a losing guild still has fun for the last 30 minutes.
  4. Resolve open ambiguities (§4) before the simulation is built.
  5. Guildhall staffing (§5) — operational, decide before the event.
